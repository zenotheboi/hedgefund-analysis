Biotech Clinical-Catalyst Strategy

Trading the stock reaction to clinical-trial and FDA outcomes, market-neutral. Perfect-foresight backtest (README Step 2) — the payoff ceiling and its honest, deployable haircut.

Bottom line. The strategy works and is market-neutral, but it is a capacity-limited sleeve, not a scalable flagship. Perfect-foresight result ~29% CAGR at Sharpe 1.65; at a realistic 90% model ~$26M (+161%). 01 The finding

  • The market prices approvals in advance — an approval is a non-event (~0% reaction).
  • The only real information event is failure: small-cap failures drop −11% to −18% in a single day.
  • Big-pharma failures don't move the stock — the tradeable signal lives in small-caps.
  • Where the money is: because ~90% of trials fail, the crowd piles in to short these names. When our model picks the ones that will succeed, we go long and lend to those shorts — collecting their fees as they lose. The profit is on the long side; the failure signal is what makes the shorts crowded (and the lending rich).

02 The strategy

Long + lend DEPLOYED

Buy known successes ~1 month out, hold ~3 months, and lend the shares to the crowded shorts for borrow-fee income. This is the engine.

Short / puts TESTED & DROPPED

Short (or buy puts on) known failures. After honest borrow limits it nets flat-to-negative — only pays in the bull regime — so it is not deployed (see §04).

Hedge beta OPTIONAL

Net biotech-sector exposure hedged with XBI — returns are alpha, not market direction. Earns nothing; a market-neutral mandate / tail-insurance choice (it cost return in this window).

Money comes from long price appreciation + lending fees. The approval price move contributes ~0; the short leg and the hedge are not return drivers.

03 Mechanics & assumptions

The levers a reviewer should interrogate, with each value's confidence.

Portfolio. Size 5% of capital per position, max 10% per company, gross cap 150% → up to ~30 positions open at once; new events queue when capital is full. reasonable, swept

Timing grounded in our data. Long: enter T−20 (the drift starts ~1 month out), hold to T+63 (~3 months, to collect borrow). Short: enter T−10, cover T+5 (the drop is a one-day gap).

Executability gate — "keep 60%" assumed · swept 30–90%. We cannot short every failure: the biggest-drop names are the hardest to borrow (smallest float, most crowded). We rank failures by drop size and drop the worst 40%, keep 60% as actually shortable. This is why the short leg barely earns — we lose exactly the best shorts.

Borrow-fee formula — the lending engine no data · swept 25–400%. Fees are a daily flow, not paid upfront; the rate collapses after the catalyst because shorts cover and demand vanishes.

Lending income = notional × rate × utilization × (days/252) × multiplier
  pre-catalyst (T−20→T0, 20 days): full rate   multiplier 1.0
  post-catalyst (T0→T+63, 63 days): collapsed  multiplier 0.10

worked example, one $1M position (rate 100%/yr, util 40%):
  pre = 1M × 1.00 × 0.40 × 20/252 × 1.00 = $31,700
  post = 1M × 1.00 × 0.40 × 63/252 × 0.10 = $10,000
  total lending = $41,700 (4.2% of the position)

Costs market convention: 80 bps market impact + 5 bps commission, round trip (small caps move on your trades).

05 Robustness (Monte Carlo)

Point estimates turned into distributions. Two different questions:

On the deployed long+lend book. We hold the strongest driver — model accuracy — fixed at a common level (90% and 80%) so two other sources of luck are compared like-for-like. Trade luck (bootstrap): re-deal the actual trades — "just a few lucky deals?" Assumption luck (Monte Carlo): re-draw the cost assumptions — borrow rate, utilization, post-catalyst borrow, impact — both independently and correlated (they co-move in a stress regime). The last row runs the same test un-hedged, so the hedge is compared as two distributions, not a random input.

At a fixed model accuracy, trade luck and assumption luck land in the same place (~$27M at 90%, ~$24M at 80%) with near-identical spreads — so the result is not a few lucky deals nor lucky cost guesses. Correlating the assumptions (a bad-liquidity regime hitting borrow and costs together) trims the upside only slightly. P(loss) is 0% everywhere — dropping the short leg removed the downside. Un-hedged sits ~$3–4M higher at both baselines: the hedge cost return in this window without cutting the tail, so it is a mandate / tail-insurance choice, not a return driver. The lever that dominates is model accuracy.

07 Recommendation

Does the strategy actually add profit? Yes — on a like-for-like basis, price-trading alone ends at $24.6M; adding the lending overlay reaches $28.9M (+18%). The lending is the differentiator, not decoration. The short leg was tested and dropped (it only pays in the bull regime); the deployed book is long+lend.

Next step: proceed to Step 3/4 — build the real predictive model and measure its true accuracy (we have only stress-tested a 70–100% range, not built it). The one number that decides everything: how well the model catches failures, so we never go long a landmine. Capacity caps AUM at ~$50–150M (small-cap liquidity), so run it as an uncorrelated sleeve inside a larger book, not a standalone flagship fund.

08 How to read this (FAQ)

How is position size decided?Fixed-fractional: 5% of capital ($500k) per name, capped 10% per company, 150% gross — not conviction-weighted. Gives ~20–30 positions for diversification.
What is the short leg — puts or borrowing?Two different ways to bet on a drop: borrow-and-short (borrow shares, pay a borrow fee, recall risk) or buy a put (pay a premium, no borrow). We tested both; the leg doesn't pay, so it's dropped. On the long side we are the lender — renting our shares to the crowd's shorts.
Why was short-leg P&L negative?The executability gate throws away our best shorts (biggest drops = hardest to borrow); what's left barely covers borrow cost + frictions. Net −$0.6M (BASE), −$1.6M (BEAR). Only positive in BULL.
"The hedge buys low drawdown"?Figurative: the XBI hedge earns nothing but is meant to buy (pay for) a smaller drawdown by cancelling biotech-sector beta. In THIS window it didn't — un-hedged had a smaller drawdown too. So it's mandate / tail-insurance, not a return or drawdown win here.
What is a 150% gross cap?Gross = sum of all position sizes (longs+shorts, ignoring sign). Cap = 150% of $10M = $15M deployed at once (1.5× leverage). New positions queue when full. Net (directional) exposure stays ~0.
80 bps impact + 5 bps commission?Round-trip trading costs. Impact 0.80%: small-caps move against your own order. Commission 0.05%: broker fee. ~$4,250 on a $500k position.
Why T−20 in the example, not T−30?T−20 (enter ~1 month out) is the chosen entry; T−30 was just the far end of the sensitivity sweep (we tested T−30/−20/−10). No contradiction.
